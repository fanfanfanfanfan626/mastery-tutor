Following rules, or learning from examples?

Make a prediction, read the feedback, then try a different context. Experience one small part of the Mastery Tutor teaching loop.

This is a prepared interactive sample, not a live AI tutor. It does not save answers or upload your input. It is not a full product trial or evidence of learning effectiveness.

Compare two examples

A rule-based filter

A programmer specifies: “If an email contains ‘free prize’, flag it as suspicious.” A person directly chooses the decision rule.

A learned filter

A model receives many labeled examples of normal email and spam. Training adjusts its parameters to use features that help classify the messages.

The difference is not complexity, programming language, or even accuracy. Ask: Did people directly specify the rule, or were parameters fitted using data?

A question to take with you

Ask how the decision process was created. Rules are directly specified; learned parameters are fitted from examples. Real applications can combine both.

Three correct answers do not establish mastery. A tutor also needs new contexts, independent explanations, and spaced review—not just one correct response.
